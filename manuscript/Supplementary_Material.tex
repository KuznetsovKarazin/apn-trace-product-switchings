\section{Finite-field conventions}

The invariant statements in the paper are basis free.  Hexadecimal values in the electronic certificates use the AES polynomial basis
\[
 \F_{256}=\F_2[z]/(z^8+z^4+z^3+z+1).
\]
The embedded subfield is
\[
 E=\F_4=\{0,1,\lambda,\lambda^2\},
 \qquad \lambda=\texttt{0xbd},
 \qquad \lambda^2+\lambda+1=0.
\]
For the projective calculation,
\[
 L=\F_{16}\subset\F_{256},\qquad r=\texttt{0xb0},\qquad r^5=1,
\]
and
\[
 \lambda=r^2+r^3,
 \qquad \lambda^2=r+r^4.
\]
The relation between the vector trace term in Arshad's dissertation and the scalar trace-product representative is verified by
\[
 \tr(y)y=\tr(y)\tr(\lambda y)+\lambda^2\tr(y),\qquad y\in E.
\]
The last summand is binary linear and therefore changes neither the polar update nor the EA class.

\section{Corrected affine character-sum bookkeeping}

Let $C_\theta:X^3+Y^3=\theta Z^3$ and let $S_{u,v}^{\mathrm{aff}}$ denote the affine character sum used to count trace-constrained representations.  For an off-axis phase, flex-tangent cancellation makes one point at infinity regular with phase value zero.  The complete-curve sum contains its character contribution $+1$, whereas the affine sum does not.  Thus
\[
 \abs{S_{u,v}^{\mathrm{aff}}}\le 4\sqrt q+1
\]
for each of the nine off-axis pairs.  Combining the Hasse error, the six axis estimates, and the nine affine off-axis estimates gives
\begin{align*}
 16h_n(\theta)
 &\ge q-2-2\sqrt q-6(6\sqrt q)-9(4\sqrt q+1)\\
 &=q-11-74\sqrt q.
\end{align*}
The numerator is positive at $q=2^{14}$ and thereafter.  The exact bridge values are
\[
 \min_{\theta\ne0}h_{10}(\theta)=46,
 \qquad
 \min_{\theta\ne0}h_{12}(\theta)=208.
\]
The correction is stored in \nolinkurl{publication_uniform_theorem_corrected.json}.  It changes an intermediate constant term only; the theorem and threshold are unchanged.

\section{Reduced projective-selector calculation}

Choose $r\in\F_{16}$ of order five and $w\in\F_{256}$ with $w^2+w=r$.  The six transitions in $\mathrm{GL}(2,2)$ split as
\[
 y\longmapsto\alpha y,
 \qquad
 y\longmapsto\alpha y^2,
 \qquad \alpha\in\F_4^*.
\]
The linear transitions are excluded by the unsolvable equation
\[
 z^2+z+1+r=0,
 \qquad \Tr_{\F_{16}/\F_2}(1+r)=1.
\]
For each semilinear involution, the normalized product multiset of the 16 reduced pairs is
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}
\toprule
product & multiplicity\\
\midrule
$r$ & 2\\
$r^2$ & 2\\
$r+r^2$ & 4\\
$r+r^3$ & 2\\
$1+r^2$ & 2\\
$r+r^2+r^3$ & 2\\
$1+r+r^2$ & 2\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The three diagonal targets are $1+r^3$, $r^4$, and $r+r^2$.  Only the last occurs, with multiplicity four.  Consequently the rejected diagonal coefficient has four bad sections for each of the three involutions, hence twelve in total.  Full representatives, roots, and hexadecimal values are stored in \nolinkurl{rank_two_selector_proof.json}.

\section{Independent validation of the hyperplane specialization}

For $F(x)=x^3$ over $\F_{16}$, all 4096 normalized binary linear maps $L$ were tested independently by:
\begin{enumerate}
\item direct differential-uniformity computation;
\item the all-direction polar-kernel criterion;
\item the H-equivalence hyperplane-injectivity criterion.
\end{enumerate}
All three tests agree for every map, and exactly 448 maps are accepted.  This calculation verifies the formal specialization while deliberately making no priority claim for the hyperplane criterion.

\section{Contents not required for the logical proof}

The archive retains several broader Stage~5 scripts and JSON files for provenance.  Permutation screens, stabilizer-orbit experiments, and the original Gr\"obner-slice discovery campaign are not used to establish the theorems in the manuscript.  They are included only when needed by a downstream validation script or to document the route by which the trace-product family was identified.
